# Customer conversations and continuous discovery

## Interview rules

- Talk about their life and the problem, not your idea. Don't pitch.
- Ask about specific past events: "When did this last happen? What did you do? What did it cost?" Never "Would you use...?" or "Is this a good idea?"
- Ask about current workarounds and money or time already spent. Spending beats praise.
- Treat compliments and future enthusiasm as weak signals. Look for commitments: a next step, an introduction, a payment, time set aside.
- Listen more than you talk. Follow up on surprises before moving on.
- Keep prep short. Talk to people this week, not after weeks of preparation.
- Keep going until new conversations stop surfacing new information. If patterns don't converge, the segment is too broad.

When drafting questions for the user, rewrite any future-tense or leading question into a past-behavior one and say why.

## Weekly discovery loop

1. At least one customer touchpoint per week, ideally with the engineer present. A quarterly research sprint is not discovery.
2. Log each interview as a snapshot: who, the story, the pain, the workaround.
3. Map opportunities (needs and pains) under the desired outcome (opportunity solution tree). Generate several solutions per opportunity.
4. For the top solution, list its assumptions and test the riskiest one first, cheaply.
5. Record decisions and reasons in a log so the team can revisit them.

Sources: Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*; Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*.
